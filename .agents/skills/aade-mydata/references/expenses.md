# Reconciling expenses

1. Retrieve `request-my-expenses` for the explicit date range, with pagination. These are book summary rows, not necessarily one row per invoice. Inspect `count`, `minMark`, `maxMark`, date/type and counterparty; do not expand aggregated rows into invented invoices.
2. Retrieve received documents for the same interval. Match financial documents to book rows by MARK where possible, then type/date/counterparty and amounts. Preserve unmatched rows and explain them.
3. If the book includes purchases not present in received documents, inspect transmitted records, optionally restricted by the invoice type shown in the book. Manually reported foreign acquisitions can appear here (for example 14.1). Use source `transmitted` as provenance only, never an income/expense classifier. Do not assume an absent purchase does not exist just because cross-border systems are not linked.
4. Resolve currency from document data; the expense-book row may not provide one. Do not assume EUR or combine currencies without an explicit conversion method. Preserve totals and unmatched records separately if currency cannot be established.
5. Reconcile credit signs explicitly. A credit can have positive amounts in its invoice XML and negative values in the expense book. Deduct it once, using document type and book evidence; do not apply absolute values or double-negate.
6. Reconcile separate `cancelledInvoicesDoc` notices with invoice MARKs as well as invoice cancellation metadata. Exclude canceled amounts where supported by the returned data, explaining the treatment. Separate delivery notes and other nonfinancial documents; do not call every returned XML invoice element an expense invoice.
7. Use Decimal for all arithmetic. Reconcile net, VAT, other taxes, fees, stamp duty, withholding, deductions and gross rather than assuming gross equals net plus VAT in all cases. Compare individual documents and summary totals; never add both sources together.
8. Summarize the recorded net/VAT/gross, financial count, credits, and optional month/supplier breakdowns. Leave supplier names unknown when the API omits them. State the date scope, source completeness, currency and any unmatched/aggregated rows.

Do not describe these amounts as cash paid, tax-deductible costs, recovered VAT, or complete accounting expenses. Foreign-purchase VAT entries can represent accounting treatment rather than money paid to the supplier. Unreported documents, delayed reporting and accounting adjustments may be absent. Preserve these limits without turning a retrieval task into unsolicited tax advice.
